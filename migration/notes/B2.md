# B2: exact discrete inverse statics + stability (heliades.sim), results

Package: `heliades/sim/inverse.py` (inverse, critical modulus, fallback) and `heliades/sim/hessian.py`
(subtree loads, gradient, exact tangent stiffness, O(n) tree factorisation / solve / Sturm count,
Newton statics). Math: `docs/sim/INVERSE.md`. Tests: `tests/heliades/test_sim_inverse.py`
(14 tests, ~13 s). Demo: `scripts/inverse_statics_demo.py` → `outputs/sim/tomato_inverse_hold.mp4`,
`outputs/sim/inverse_summary.json`.

**Method.** The tree is statically determinate. One leaf→root segmented sum gives every joint's
moment m_j = R_jᵀN_j at the target. A per-joint 3×3 Newton solves B1's own quaternion spring law
2Γ(Q)Kv = m_j (small-angle branch), and R0rel_j = R_rel,target,j R(Q_j)ᵀ. Stability comes from the
exact joint-space Hessian including gravity's geometric stiffness. It has ancestor structure, so a
leaf→root block elimination with 3×3 pivots factors it in O(n), and by Sylvester/Haynsworth the
count of negative pivots is the count of unstable modes (a Sturm count). Shifts give λ_min and the
buckling pencil (H, K_el) → E_cr.

**Change to B1 (additive).** `TreeSim.R0rel` is now a per-batch attribute (default: the topology's
rest, broadcast) and `TreeSim.set_rest(R0rel)` replaces it. `_kinematics` reads `self.R0rel`
instead of `T.R0rel`. The B1 tests are unchanged and pass. Rest rotations must be per batch (they
depend on E) and must carry twist, which node positions cannot represent.

### Tomato critical modulus, and B1's 2e8

| what must hold the Helios shape | E_cr [Pa] |
|---|---|
| whole plant (exact inverse + Sturm) | **7.32e8** |
| linearised generalized eigenproblem, at E = 1e9 / at 5e8 / at E_cr | 7.24e8 / 7.45e8 / 7.32e8 |
| all stems (petioles/rachis ×1e3 stiffer) | 3.36e8 |
| main axis only (everything else ×1e3 stiffer) | **2.38e8** |
| B1 forward estimate (sag amplification, Greenhill with base radius) | ≈ 2e8 |

B1's 2e8 is the main-axis self-weight buckling mode. Our main-axis-only value, 2.38e8, agrees
with it. Holding the *whole* Helios shape needs 3× more. The binding modes are:
1. petioles whose leaflets sit above the joint: spring-held inverted pendula, made worse by the
   quaternion spring's softening at the 0.5–1 rad per-joint rest angles the inverse needs on
   2-segment petioles;
2. leaf-laden inclined side shoots (subtrees of 7–64 bodies).

A forward settle never sees these modes. The leaves droop into a stable hanging configuration
instead, which is why B1 found stable (sagged) equilibria down to 1e8. At the minimum stable
modulus, the generalized margins are μ_min = 0.276 at 1e9 and −0.49 at 5e8. The smallest Hessian
eigenvalue is +1.9e-4 N·m/rad at 1e9 and −7.6e-3 at 5e8; it is dominated by the softest leaflets,
which is why the K-metric margin is the more informative number.

Fallback at 3e8: 213 rounds, 32 s, 442 joints stiffened by more than ×1.05, max ×5.7. That rules
out "uniform E" for this asset at 3e8.

|---|---|---|
| inverse statics only | 1.8 ms | 66 ms (1.0 ms/member) |
| inverse + stability (Sturm count) | **3.3 ms** | **120 ms** (1.9 ms/member) |
| + λ_min and μ_min by Sturm bisection (≈2 × 60 counts) | ~0.2 s | |
| exact critical E (5 rounds × 16 moduli as one batch) | ~1 s | |
| dense `eigh` cross-check (1428 dof) | ~1 s | |

B = 1 is bound by per-level Python overhead (27 levels × 2 sweeps), and B = 64 by stacked 3×3
`eigh`/`inv`. Both sweeps use the same segmented reductions as B1 and port directly to Warp.

## Findings worth keeping

1. **An exact inverse equilibrium is not a usable rest shape until its stability is checked.**
   At E = 5e8 the inverse is exact, and an unperturbed 3 s run holds the target to 1e-12 m. A
   1e-3 rad/s kick moves it 0.43 m. A hold test alone would have passed it.
2. **The binding constraint for the Helios tomato is local**, in petioles and side shoots, not the
   main-stem Greenhill mode. The leaf-to-root pivots localise it exactly (`joint_negative`).
3. **Feasibility is a separate failure mode**: the quaternion spring has a torque capacity
   (k sin θ). Below ~3e8 several thin petiolules cannot hold their leaflet at the Helios angle at all.
4. Newton statics with the O(n) tree solve converges B1's equilibria to 1e-16 in 2–3 iterations
   (`newton_static`). It is a better `solve_static` near buckling too, as long as the equilibrium is stable.

## Limitations

* Dead loads only, no contact/ground in the inverse. Rigid leaves (a leaf-shell inverse is
  internally indeterminate and needs a Hsu-style global solve).
* Exact for the *discrete* model. Petioles with 2 segments at ~1 rad per joint make E_cr and
  feasibility discretisation-sensitive. Edge refinement (B1 open problem 1) should come before
  biological conclusions.
* The fallback is greedy per subtree, not a global optimum (an SDP formulation is possible).
* `heliades/sim/__init__.py` does not re-export the B2 API. Import from `heliades.sim.inverse` /
  `heliades.sim.hessian`.

## Recommended next package

**Contact + ground (Baraff O(kn) / Delassus PGS on the tree factorisation).** It is now the main
gap in both directions. Forward: the E < E_cr plants fall through the ground. Inverse: a staked or
leaf-on-leaf-supported real plant is exactly the case where a contact inverse is needed, and stakes
are how real tomatoes satisfy E < E_cr. The B2 tree factorisation (pivots P_j, articulated
stiffness Φ_j) is the operator a Schur-complement contact solve needs, for the static/inverse
problem as well as the dynamic one. Do segment refinement first (cheap). Leave the Warp port until
contact fixes the data layout. Leaf shells come after that.
