# B3: contact + ground on the tree solver, segment refinement (heliades.sim), results

Package: `heliades/sim/contact.py` (colliders, detection, O(k·depth + k²) Delassus operator from the
tree factorisation, PGS with an isotropic Coulomb cone, step hook, penalty baseline),
`contact_wp.py` (Warp PGS kernel), `refine.py` (edge subdivision), `inverse_contact.py`
(contact-aware inverse statics), `scenarios.py` (shared set-ups). Edits are additive, and all B1/B2
tests pass unchanged:
* `tree.py`: floating bases, stored per-step factorisation (`_Factor`), the bias-only O(n)
  `TreeSim.response`, collider routing (`colliders=`, `contact_params=`, `sim.contact`), and
  `BodyForce`;
* `topology.py`: floating bases, `depth` and the ancestor table `anc`;
* `hessian.py`: `BodyForce` dead loads, and a guard against floating trees.

Design and math: `docs/sim/CONTACT.md`. Tests: `tests/heliades/test_sim_contact.py` (19 tests,
about 60 s). Demo: `scripts/contact_demo.py` → `outputs/sim/tomato_ground_rest.mp4`,
`outputs/sim/tomato_finger_push.mp4`, `outputs/sim/contact_summary.json`.

**Formulation.** Velocity-level contact (Stewart–Trinkle/Anitescu) on B1's *own* implicit matrix
`M~`. B1 factorises the tree once per step. The k×k Delassus operator `W = J M~⁻¹ Jᵀ` is built
from the stored factors by one up/down sweep per contact along its root path, plus a pulled-back
row ρ and a lowest-common-ancestor gather: O(k·depth + k²) instead of Baraff's O(kn), and exact
(1e-15 vs brute-force columns). PGS runs on the 3k impulses: normal projection, then the exact
local disk QP in the W_tt metric (isotropic, non-associated Coulomb cone, maximal dissipation), with
warm start and an impulse safeguard; a Warp kernel does the inner loop. The impulses are
back-substituted through one bias-only O(n) sweep of the same factorisation.

### Segment refinement and the critical modulus

`refine_asset(asset, max_length, min_per_organ, factor)`. Original nodes keep their indices, so

| 4 | 1904 | 108 | 8.408e8 | −1.9% | 0.17 |
| 6 | 2856 | 162 | 8.517e8 | −0.6% | 0.12 |
| 8 | 3808 | 216 | **8.569e8** | 0 | 0.10 |
| Richardson (p = 1, ×6/×8) | | | **8.73e8** | | |

Observed order ≈ 1.1. The controlled cases separate the causes. A kinked chain converges at
**O(1/m²)** (order 1.99, 2.00). A single Y junction converges at **O(1/m)** (0.85 → 0.93). B1's joint
compliance counts the parent's last half segment once per child, which is exact on chains and
O(l) wrong at branch points; refinement removes it. The unrefined B2 value underestimates the
tomato's E_cr by about 15%. B2's conclusions (local petiole and side-shoot modes; E_cr ≫ the
main-axis 2.4e8) stand. With refinement ×4 the cost per inverse plus Sturm count is ×4 (O(n)).

## Performance (CPU, numpy + Warp PGS kernel, 1 thread, tomato on the ground)

Tomato E = 1e8 lying on the ground, still settling after 8 s (so PGS mostly runs to its cap of
200 sweeps: the worst case), 61 contacts per env, h = 1/60. PGS on the Warp CPU kernel. Breakdown
per step, all envs:

| B | ms/step, no contact | ms/step, with contact | env-steps/s | detect | Delassus | PGS | back-sub |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4.7 | **18.9** | 53 | 1.0 | 5.3 | 7.0 | 0.8 |
| 16 | 28 | 203 | 79 | 13 | 65 | 92 | 3.8 |
| 64 | 105 | 792 | 81 | 61 | 259 | 350 | 12.5 |
| 1, numpy PGS | | 2163 | 0.46 | | | 2150 | |

* At B = 1 contact costs 4× a contact-free step and runs at about 0.9× real time (h = 1/60).
  Quasi-static scenes converge in 1–30 sweeps with warm start (finger: about 3 ms per step of
  contact work). The chain resting on the ground needs 1 sweep.
* The Delassus cost is O(k·depth + k²) in numpy and dominated by the k² gather. PGS is
  O(sweeps·k²) per env, and the Warp CPU backend runs the envs serially. Both scale linearly in B,
  hence the flat ≈ 80 env-steps/s. On the GPU, both are one block per env (see the B4 section).
* The Warp kernel is about 300× faster than the numpy reference PGS (identical result).

## Findings worth keeping

1. **The naive "unconstrained 2×2 solve + radial clamp" friction step is not Coulomb.** It is a
   Euclidean projection, so at the limit λ_t is antiparallel to W_tt⁻¹v_t, not to v_t. On a rigid
   block, PGS converged to 1e-14 to a *spurious sliding* solution at tan φ = 0.98μ (yaw plus slip,
   Ft/Fn = 0.44). The exact local disk QP in the W_tt metric (secular equation) fixes it: stick to
   1e-11 m, and slip acceleration exact.
2. **The penalty baseline is not usable on the tomato at h = 1/60.** The linearly-implicit penalty
   ground diverges for k = 5e2 … 2e4 N/m at h = 1/60 and 1/240, and is stable only at h = 1/1000.
   Light leaflets on near-flaccid petiolules hit the ground at 1–2 m/s, and the spring is
   linearised about q_n while the leaflet rotates by about 1 rad per step. Implicit contact has no
   stiffness to linearise, and it is stable at 1/60 with 0.05 mm residual penetration.
3. **Inconsistent penetration constraints break PGS.** A leaflet inside a sphere has vertex normals
   on all sides. Asking all of them to separate is infeasible, the impulses grow, the leaflets spin
   up (hω ≈ 1), W turns strongly non-symmetric and indefinite, and PGS diverges (residual 1e131 by
   step 3). The fix has three parts: deep contacts in curved colliders recover slowly
   (half-spaces are always consistent); a generous per-contact impulse cap; and a step-level
   velocity guard, which scales the impulses by α ≤ 1 so that no contact velocity exceeds about
   twice the free velocity plus the target. The per-contact cap alone failed once reduction was
   off: about 105 inconsistent contacts per member couple through off-diagonal W. A converged
   solve has α = 1. Also, the speculative band must be capped (2 cm): whipping leaflets otherwise
   flood the contact set, and the K² assembly ran out of memory.
4. **W is non-symmetric in motion.** B1's linearised-implicit velocity-product terms are O(hω) and
   reach 40–100% on whipping leaflets; at rest they vanish. We keep the exact operator, because
   the symmetric part would desynchronise the predicted and the integrated contact velocities.
5. **Friction decides whether a stake holds a soft plant.** At E = 1e8 the stem slides sideways off
   a round stake with μ = 0.4 and stays upright with μ = 1. Real staking uses ties. A bilateral tie
   constraint is the same machinery with a two-sided λ.
6. **Bushy canopies intersect any vertical stake.** A collider feature filter is needed
   (`stake(..., bodies=main_axis(sim))`).
7. **Contact reduction hurts more than it helps on plants.** Keeping 5 hull points per leaflet let
   interior vertices of curved Helios leaflets sink: 1.3 mm late penetration, against 0.005 mm
   without reduction, for only 61 vs 53 contacts and the same wall time. It is now off by default.
   Grouping per host body was worse still, because 2–4 leaflets share a petiole body and whole
   leaflets sank.

## Limitations

* No self-contact (leaf–leaf, leaf–stem) and no plant–plant contact. These need a broad phase and
  two-path Jacobian rows; the Delassus formula extends with two LCAs per pair.
* Leaves are vertex point clouds, so a finger smaller than about 1 cm can pass between vertices.
  Kinematic colliders translate only.
* PGS can cycle in violent transients with redundant contacts (iteration cap 200). The step stays
  bounded, and the residual shows up as transient penetration (4 mm at a 2 m/s impact). A semismooth
  Newton or interior-point method on k would give guaranteed residuals.
* `supported_inverse`: dense stability check (O(n³)), the collider's surface curvature neglected,
  forces chosen by minimal prestress (a modelling choice), B = 1. Floating bases cannot be inverted.
* Junction compliance (Finding / refinement) is a B1 modelling choice, kept for backward
  compatibility. A shared-compliance junction would make the unrefined model second order.

## What the Warp port (B4) should fix in the data layout

1. Keep the per-step factorisation resident on device: `UD`, `W`, `D⁻¹`, `X'`, `S'` per body.
   Contact reads them twice (Delassus, back-substitution). Store `X'` as `r` plus two 3×3 h-blocks,
   not a full 6×6.
2. Use compacted, ragged contact arrays with per-member offsets (a prefix sum after detection)
   instead of (B, K_max) padding. Give each member its own dense W block: shared memory for
   K ≤ 64, global memory above that.
3. Run the path sweeps one thread per contact over the int32 ancestor table `anc[nb, nlev]`, and
   assemble the k² blocks one thread per pair. Run PGS one warp per member, with the column update
   parallel over rows. `contact_wp._pgs_kernel` already has this structure.
4. Do contact reduction as a segmented argmax over (member, group, collider) keys, not a lexsort.
5. Use float64 for W and PGS: the leaf/stem W_nn ratio is about 10⁴.
