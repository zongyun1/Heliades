# B1: forward tree direct solver (heliades.sim), results

Package: `heliades/sim/` (`tree.py` solver, `topology.py` asset→level-ordered tree, `rotation.py`,
`analytic.py` references). Design: `docs/sim/DESIGN.md`. Tests: `tests/heliades/test_sim_tree.py`
(14 tests, ~28 s; the 15th, the XPBD contrast (~60 s), is gated by `HELIADES_SLOW=1`). Demo: `scripts/sim_tree_demo.py`
→ `outputs/sim/tomato_settle_tree.mp4`, `outputs/sim/tomato_tree_vs_xpbd.mp4`, `outputs/sim/summary.json`.

**Formulation.** Reduced coordinates: one rigid body per rod segment, a 3-DOF spherical joint per
segment, inextensible. Joint energy U = 2 vᵀKv on the deviation quaternion (= ½φᵀKφ + O(θ⁴)),
K = diag(EI, EI, GJ)/l*. Linearly-implicit Euler for springs, damping, velocity-product terms,
gravity geometric stiffness (PSD part) and penalty contacts. Every term is joint- or body-local,
so the articulated-body algorithm solves each step **exactly in O(n)**: three level sweeps,
batched over B parameter sets.

## 6. Helios tomato 75 d (`build_helios_plant("tomato", 75, seed=0)`)

Note: the current pyhelios build (third_party, 0.1.43.dev0) produces a **larger tomato than the
README table**: 478 rod nodes, 497 leaflets, 0 fruit, height 0.83 m, 0.55 kg (README: 129 nodes,
112 leaflets, 6 fruit). The "+6 × 11 g fruit" rows add fruit on 2 cm, r = 1.2 mm pedicels hanging
from upper stem nodes (`analytic.with_hanging_fruit`) to recreate the original stress case.
Rod tree: 476 bodies, **27 levels**. Leaflet petiolules have r = 0.18–1.2 mm (median 0.6 mm).

Dynamic settle, h = 1/30, mass-proportional damping α = 5/s, 3 s simulated, B = 5 in one batch:

| E [Pa] | settled (< 1 mm/s) | t_settle | max |v| at 3 s | stem-top sag (% of 0.83 m) | max node disp | static solve sag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e8 | no (folds over) | – | 20 mm/s | 33.2% | 880 mm | **45.2%** (stable, post-buckled) |
| 2e8 | no (slow mode) | – | 125 mm/s | 19.9% | 711 mm | 18.7% |
| 3e8 | no (slow mode) | – | 41 mm/s | 13.0% | 578 mm | 12.8% |
| 5e8 | **yes** | **2.43 s** | 0.12 mm/s | **3.32%** | 360 mm | 3.32% |
| 1e9 | **yes** | **2.03 s** | 0.04 mm/s | **1.00%** | 172 mm | 1.00% |
| 5e8 + 6 × 11 g fruit | **yes** | 2.37 s | 0.19 mm/s | 7.31% | 424 mm | |
| 1e9 + 6 × 11 g fruit | **yes** | 2.07 s | 0.05 mm/s | 1.58% | 198 mm | |

How to read this:
* **Sag is bounded and E-dependent, with no creep.** After settling, 1 more second of simulation
  moves nothing by more than 2 mm (test). A perturbed dynamic run from the `solve_static`
  equilibria stays within 1 mm, so every row has a *stable* equilibrium.
* **E ≈ 2e8 Pa is this plant's self-weight buckling modulus.** The sag amplification
  1 % → 3.3 % → 12.8 % → 18.7 % → 45 % (1e9 → 1e8) follows a P-δ factor 1/(1 − E_cr/E) with
  E_cr ≈ 2e8. The Greenhill estimate for a column under self-weight (base r = 8.8 mm, E = 1e8,
  0.55 kg over 0.83 m) gives a critical height of 0.83 m, which is the plant height. Below E_cr the
  tomato folds over to a post-buckled equilibrium (45% sag). The fall is h-independent
  (h = 1/60 vs 1/240: stem-top height differs by 0.3% of H after 2 s, test). It is a property of
  the asset (Helios radii) and the modulus, not of the solver. Real tomato plants are often staked.
* **E = 2e8–3e8 does not settle in 3 s** because of a physical soft mode near the buckling point
  (≈ 0.26 Hz, ω ≈ 1.6 rad/s). With mass-proportional damping, its decay rate is at most ≈ ω
  (critical damping), so going from 0.4 m/s to 1 mm/s takes ≥ 3.7 s for any α. `solve_static`
  (pseudo-time modified Newton, same fixed point) reaches all five in ≤ 260 iterations
  (~1–2 s wall for B = 5).
* **Max displacement is dominated by leaflets.** With petiolule radii of 0.18–0.6 mm and
  E ≤ 1e9, EI is so small that leaflets hang like pendulums: about 1 rad per joint at E = 1e8.
  The Helios shape already contains kinematic droop, so this double-counts gravity
  (inverse statics, a later package). The stem-only max displacement is 126 mm at E = 1e9.

## Energy and numerical damping (test 8 and quantification)

Zero damping, no gravity, cantilever first mode (3.36 Hz) released from a static deflection:

| h | E(10 s)/E0 | energy kept per cycle | ζ_num measured | ωh/2 predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1/60 | 5e-31 | 0.125 | 0.166 | 0.176 |
| 1/240 | 1e-8 | 0.577 | 0.044 | 0.044 |
| 1/1000 | 1.1e-2 | 0.875 | 0.0106 | 0.0105 |

Tomato, random initial joint velocities, h = 1/60: E(10 s)/E0 = 3.0e-3, monotone. Backward Euler
dissipates with exactly the textbook ζ ≈ ωh/2, and **never gains energy**. Before the
velocity-product terms were made implicit, the same test diverged (energy rate +h·ω², tomato
leaflets at 100 rad/s), and the tomato drop exploded at h = 1/60 for E = 1e8.

## Performance (CPU, numpy, 1 thread, tomato 476 bodies + 497 leaves)

| B | steps/s | env-steps/s | ms/step |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ~210 (190–250 across runs) | 210 | 4.8 |
| 16 | ~35 | 560 | 28 |
| 64 | ~9 | 560 | 115 |

At h = 1/60, B = 1 runs at about 3.5× real time. Batching saturates at about 550–640 env-steps/s:
B = 1 is bound by per-level Python overhead (27 levels × 3 sweeps), and large B is compute-bound
on stacked 6×6 products in numpy. The implicit velocity and gravity terms cost 15–25%; turning them
off is faster but unstable on this plant. For comparison, XPBD needs 3840 substeps per simulated
second for this plant and still does not converge (about 0.025 s simulated per wall second).
Next speed-ups: Warp kernels (fused per-level work; launch dimension B × level width), or a
process pool over the batch on CPU.

## Open problems

1. **Leaflet petiolules are near-flaccid** with Helios radii. Leaflets hang at ~1 rad per joint
   (E = 1e8), so the per-joint angle is large and the sin θ softening of the joint energy matters.
   Needed: segment refinement (subdivide asset edges, cheap in O(n)) and a radius sanity check
   for Helios petioles/rachis (likely a visual radius, not a structural one).
2. **Inverse statics** (Helios shapes include droop). With this solver it is an O(n) leaf-to-root
   sum of distal loads, then a local quaternion solve per joint (DESIGN §7). The buckling
   result above shows it must also check stability (E < E_cr has no upright solution at all).
3. **Slow settling near buckling** is physical. Decide whether settle = `solve_static` (statics) or
   damped dynamics (videos). Real2Sim fits around E ~ 2–3e8 will be stiff, ill-conditioned
   problems.
4. **No ground plane / self-contact.** At E = 1e8 the folded plant passes through the ground.
   Contact is next (Baraff O(kn) Schur complement on this factorisation).
5. **Numerical damping** at h = 1/60 (ζ ≈ ωh/2). For dynamics fidelity use h ≤ 1/500 or add BDF2.
6. **Rigid leaves.** Shell leaves plug in as condensed child bodies (DESIGN §7).
7. The README "tomato 75 d" numbers (129 nodes, 6 fruit) no longer match the current pyhelios
   build (478 nodes, 0 fruit). The README table should be regenerated.
8. `heliades.sim` is not yet in `pyproject.toml`'s `[tool.setuptools] packages` (outside this
   package's allowed files). Tests import it through `pythonpath = ["."]`.

## Recommended next package

**B2: contact + ground on the tree factorisation** (Baraff O(kn) / Delassus PGS), together with
**inverse statics** (O(n), uses the same leaf-to-root sweep), then the **Warp port**. Inverse
statics is the highest-value next step scientifically: the forward solver is now exact and fast
enough that `settle(rest) == observed` can be tested to round-off, which no prior plant system
has done. Leaf shells (condensed child bodies) come after, once contact exists.
